What we do

We build educational technology and conduct the research that informs it — starting with iSkool, a school management platform built for the realities of South African high schools.

Our mission

"Advancing education through research and technology — for every school, not just the well-resourced ones."

South Africa's education system is not broken for lack of effort. It is strained by scale, by history, and by the gap between what technology has been built to do and what the average public school actually needs. ENTHROPY exists to close that gap — by building tools grounded in research, designed for the schools that need them most.

Why we exist

The problem is bigger than any single tool can fix.

We read the data. We talk to teachers. Then we build — carefully, and only where software can actually help.

Read our research

Of the 1.2 million learners who started Grade 1 in 2013, only 615,000 passed matric in 2024. The real throughput rate — not the headline pass rate — is around 51%. That number has not improved meaningfully in years. In many respects, it is getting worse.

Behind that number is a set of overlapping crises: a teacher vacancy rate that leaves remaining educators managing classes of 50 or more; an administrative burden that eats hours that should go to teaching; a parental communication gap that leaves families locked out of what's happening at school; and a digital divide that means the tools built for better-resourced environments simply don't work here.

Software cannot fix poverty, infrastructure, or decades of underinvestment. We are clear about that. What software can do — carefully, modestly, and built around the actual constraints of under-resourced schools — is reduce the friction that causes learners who are still enrolled to quietly disengage. That is the thread we are pulling.

ENTHROPY was started because the people building educational technology for South Africa were mostly not building it for the schools where it was needed most. We wanted to change that — by starting with the research, understanding the real conditions, and building tools that work within them.

How we work

Research first. Technology second. Always in that order.

01

We read the data before we write the code

Every product decision at ENTHROPY starts with research — published studies, government data, conversations with educators. We do not assume we know what schools need. We find out.

02

We build for constraint, not ideal conditions

Unstable internet. Shared devices. Teachers managing 55 learners on their own. These are not edge cases — they are the median condition. We design for the school that actually exists, not the one that should.

03

We publish what we learn

The research we do informs our products. It also informs the field. We share what we find — because the problem is bigger than ENTHROPY, and better information helps everyone working on it.

What we believe

The principles we hold ourselves to.

Honesty about what tech can do
Software does not solve poverty, infrastructure failure, or curriculum gaps. We are clear about that — in our research, in our product, and in how we talk about both. We build for the problem technology can actually touch, and we do not oversell what that is.
Built for the teacher in the room
The teacher managing 55 learners with no support staff, unstable connectivity, and a departmental return due on Friday is our primary user. Not the administrator. Not the school governing body. The person in the room, teaching.
Research is not a marketing tool
We publish our research because the field needs it — not to back up decisions we have already made. If the data tells us something we do not want to hear, we publish that too. The problem is too serious for confirmation bias.
Long-term thinking
Education does not move at the pace of a product sprint. We are patient investors in the problem — building tools that last, publishing research that compounds, and measuring success in learner outcomes, not feature velocity.
Context is everything
A tool built for a well-resourced school in one country is not automatically transferable to an under-resourced school in another. We ground every decision in the specific realities of South African public education — because that is where we work, and who we work for.
The company
